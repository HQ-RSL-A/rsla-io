## Two kinds of website projects

**No website yet.** Some owners come to us with no website at all. A Facebook page, a Google listing, maybe a logo a cousin made. Customers find them anyway, then go quiet because there is nowhere to check what the business does.

**A site from 2018.** Others have a site that has not been touched since 2018. It loads slowly on a phone, the person who built it is gone, and nobody knows the login. That project is a rebuild, and it has its own rules about keeping the rankings you already have. We cover those on the [website redesign](/services/websites/redesign) page.

Both projects get the same foundation. The difference is where we start.

## What a website has to do

If a site does not tell a stranger what the business does in one screen, it does not work, however good it looks. So the three jobs come in order.

**Say what you do.** What you do, who you do it for, where, and what happens next, on the first screen.

**Design that says it.** The layout has to carry that message, which is a different job from writing it.

**Read well to machines.** Google, and now the AI assistants people ask for recommendations, read the same page a customer does.

Most small business websites fail the first job. They open with a slogan, bury the phone number, and never say the city.

## What small business website design includes

**Copy first.** We write it from a call with you, because the words decide the layout and not the other way round. The words are also what make the site yours; a template supplies everything except them.

**Design, then a hand coded build.** We hand code every site instead of using a drag and drop builder, which is why our pages pass Google's [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) on a phone and why you keep full control of structure and speed later.

**Search structure built in.** Every site ships with one page per service, one keyword per page, proper titles and headings, schema markup, and plain question and answer sections that AI assistants can lift. For a local business, that last part is what gets you named when a customer asks ChatGPT instead of Google; the details are in [AEO for local businesses](/blog/aeo-for-local-businesses). Forms connect to your inbox or your [CRM](/services/crm) so a lead never sits unread. We hand over the domain, the code, and the CMS access, and we train you on the CMS before we leave.

## Built for what the site has to do

A service business needs a page for each service, because that is how people search and how Google ranks. A one offer business needs a landing page that does one thing well. A studio needs a portfolio that loads 40 images without stalling. A clinic needs booking.

We ask what the site is for before we open a design tool. Sometimes the answer is a three page site and a strong Google Business Profile, and that is the proposal you get.

## Questions people ask before hiring us

### How much does small business website design cost?

One fixed price for the whole project, quoted after one call, and it does not move once you sign. What moves the number is the page count, whether the site needs booking, online ordering, or custom functionality, and how much existing content has to be migrated. A 5 page site for a plumber and a 40 page site with a store are different projects, and the proposal says which one you need.

### How long does it take to build a website?

4 to 6 weeks from kickoff to launch for most projects. A focused single page site can ship faster. A larger site with custom functionality can take up to 8 weeks. The timeline is in the proposal before we start.

### Do I own my website?

Yes. Domain, code, content, and CMS access are all yours the day the project closes. There is no vendor lock in and no monthly fee to keep what you paid for.

### Can I just build it myself with Wix or an AI builder?

You can, and it may look fine. The problem is not the look. Template builders share layouts across hundreds of businesses, limit your control over speed and structure, and the AI builders skip the keyword plan, the page structure, and the content that gets a site found. For some stages a builder is the right call, and then the first call ends there, with no proposal.

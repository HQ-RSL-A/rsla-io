## Two search results, two different games

Type "plumber" into Google from your phone and you get two things: the map pack with three businesses, and the regular results underneath. They are ranked by different systems.

The map pack is your Google Business Profile: its category, its reviews, its distance from the searcher. The regular results are your website: a page for that service in that city, and the links pointing at it. Winning one does not win the other, which is why so many "local SEO packages" post to your profile every week and never move anything.

We work both, in the order that matters. Our guide to [ranking higher on Google Maps](/blog/how-to-rank-higher-on-google-maps-a-comprehensive-guide) explains the map side in full if you want the long version first.

## What actually ranks a Google Business Profile

The factors are measured every year by [Whitespark's Local Search Ranking Factors](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) survey, and the 2026 edition is blunt. The primary category is factor one of 187. Reviews, their recency, and a steady flow of them are the fastest rising signals. Keywords in the services field jumped from factor 110 to 35 in a single year. Google posts sit at 148.

So that is our order. Primary category read off the top three competitors in your market, not guessed. Every predefined service Google offers for your category, plus custom ones under the names customers actually search. Then a review process that runs every week: a direct link sent after the job, no gating, no incentives, and a reply from you within 48 hours. Then hours, attributes, description, and 8 to 12 real photos, because Google's Ask Maps answers questions straight from those fields now that Q&A is gone. The field by field detail is in our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-guide-2026).

We do not geotag photos. Google strips the location data on upload and the published tests show no effect. We do not stuff the business name with keywords, because that is the top cause of suspensions. And we do not post for the sake of posting.

## What ranks your website locally

A dedicated page for each service you sell, written for your city, is the number one local organic factor. Not a homepage that lists nine services, not a city page with the name swapped in. A real page, with your work, your reviews from that city, and an answer to the question at the top.

We build those pages, wire them into your profile, and link them from the pages on your site that already get traffic. If the site itself is too old or too slow to carry them, we rebuild it; see [website design for small businesses](/services/websites). The site wide work that sits under all of this is on our [SEO services](/services/seo) page.

## Storefronts, service areas, and several towns

A storefront shows its address and ranks around it. A service area business, the plumber or landscaper who works from home, hides the address and lists the towns it serves, and it ranks strongest near home. Both are legitimate; pretending to be the other one is the second most common reason a profile gets suspended.

If you serve several towns, we do not build a page for every town times every service. We start with the smaller towns nobody else is targeting, one page per town, each with something real about that place and your work there. A landscaper serving five towns gets five pages over five weeks, not fifty in a weekend, because Google reads a batch of near identical pages as a doorway scheme and buries the whole domain.

## Citations, done once

Citations are your name, phone, and address on the directories that matter: Google, Apple, Bing, Facebook, Yelp, then the industry ones for your trade. We build 25 to 40 of them, by hand, from one canonical record, and then stop. Diminishing returns start there. We never rent citations on a monthly sync, because when the subscription ends, most of the listings decay with it.

## Reviews without the risk

Reviews gate entry to the map pack, and nearly half of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20. So the review ask has to become a habit, not a campaign. We set up the automation: one message after each job, a direct unfiltered link to your profile, a reply template for you, and nothing that pre screens the customer's mood first. Google rewrote its review policy in 2026, and the FTC fines for fake or gated reviews run to $53,088 per violation. Doing it properly is not optional anymore.

## Showing up when they ask an AI instead

More people ask ChatGPT or Google's AI for "a good electrician near me" than a year ago. Those answers are built from the pages that already rank and from the directories and lists the models read, so most of the work above counts twice. The rest is format: an answer at the top of each page in plain text, and real mentions on the review sites. We wrote up the specifics in [AEO for local businesses](/blog/aeo-for-local-businesses).

## Questions people ask about local SEO

### How much should I pay for local SEO?

It depends on how far you are from the pack today. A business with a verified profile, a decent site, and a handful of reviews needs a one off setup plus a light monthly retainer for the review process and the grid tracking. A business starting from nothing needs the profile built, a page per service, and citations first. Either way you get one number after the first call, and the scan that shows where you are starting from is free.

### How long does local SEO take?

Profile changes can move rankings within days; adding keyword named services has shown movement inside 72 hours in published tests. Reviews compound over 60 to 90 days. Website pages take three to six months. We set gates at day 30, 60, and 90 and tell you at each one what moved and what did not.

### I work from home. Can I still rank without showing my address?

Yes, as a service area business with the address hidden, which is what Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) require if customers do not visit you. The trade off is real: hidden address profiles rank strongest near home and weaker across the metro. We compensate with the website pages, links, and reviews, and we never fake a storefront address.

### Do Google posts help me rank?

Barely. Posting frequency is factor 148 of 187 and posting quantity is 168. Posts are useful as fresh material for Ask Maps and as a place to put a call to action, so we schedule one a week, but if the posts are the whole plan, the plan is wrong.
